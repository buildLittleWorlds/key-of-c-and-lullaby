What to notice

In Rulebook, the off-key notes are simply gone — struck out, unplayable. There is no wrong note because wrong notes don't exist. The result is always “correct” and a little airless.

In Trained, every key still plays — but the shading shows which notes the lullaby examples leaned on. Hit Hear a lullaby phrase and watch which keys light: the ones the tune keeps landing on are the bright ones. That's the whole training set, made audible. Nothing's forbidden here; some things are just likelier. You can break the mood. In Rulebook you can't even find the door.

Keep it honest — what this really shows vs. only suggests

What it really shows: the difference between a hard constraint (a note is removed) and a soft weight (a note is merely favoured). That gap is real, and you can hear it: the rulebook can't be surprising, the trained ear can.

What it only suggests: that this is how a “trained model” works. It isn't, quite. The weights here are just a tally — I counted how often each scale-degree appears in three public-domain lullabies (Brahms, Twinkle, Rock-a-bye) and shaded the keys to match. That's a histogram, not a network. A real learned model weighs notes in context (what came before), holds thousands of such odds at once, and would happily suggest an out-of-key note if the examples did. This toy has no memory of your last note at all.

The honest punchline: push far enough and the rule dissolves into the vibe. “Only scale notes” is really just “these notes got weight 1, the rest weight 0” — the hardest possible version of a trained preference. Law is habit with the volume turned all the way up. Your ear runs the soft version constantly; the rulebook is the rare moment it goes rigid.
